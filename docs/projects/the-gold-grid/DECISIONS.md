# The Gold Grid: decisions

Newest at the bottom. Each entry says what was decided and why. To change one, add a new entry; do not edit the old one.

| Date | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 26 Nov 2026 | Teach the "why" with every method, not just examples. | The owner's view: understanding sticks better than a worked example alone. |
| 26 Nov 2026 | Questions are generated by code, with wrong answers built from real mistakes. | No question bank to write, the quiz never runs out, and each wrong answer can be explained. |
| 26 Nov 2026 | Start as multiple choice, then make the learner produce the answer. | Recalling an answer builds stronger memory than recognising one. |
| 26 Nov 2026 | Sell to the parent, not just teach the child. | Free and well-funded products already cover practice. Telling a parent which mistake their child keeps making is the gap. |
| 26 Nov 2026 | Three tiers: free with no AI and no sign-up; paid memory layer; paid premium AI with limits. | The free tier costs almost nothing to run and can be shared widely. AI costs money per use, so it sits in the top tier. |
| 26 Nov 2026 | Tier 2 includes separate parent and child sign-ons, and progress graphed for the parent. | They need to see different things. The child profile uses a PIN and no email, to hold as little data about a child as possible. |
| 26 Nov 2026 | Drop grades. Start with one skill: times tables. | No curriculum or country mapping needed, a wide audience, and later maths depends on it. |
| 26 Nov 2026 | Facts up to 10 × 10, with 11 and 12 as a bonus level. Division second. Aimed at children with parents. Free version first. | Agreed as a set, to keep the first version small. |
| 26 Nov 2026 | Levels follow the tricks, not table order: ×1 and ×10, ×2, ×5, turn it around, ×4, ×9, ×3, ×6, ×8, squares, ×7, mixed, bonus. | Each trick builds on one already learned. |
| 26 Nov 2026 | A fact is solid after three right answers without a hint, with at least five other questions between each. | "Three separate occasions" was the agreed idea. Spacing within a sitting lets a child see gold squares on day one. May be tightened to separate days later. |
| 26 Nov 2026 | All levels are open; the next one is marked "Up next". A level is passed at 7 of the last 8. | A parent may want a specific table. No automatic moving down. |
| 27 Nov 2026 | Facts with a 9 use "×10, take one away" as the main trick, ahead of the ×5 half trick. A second way is shown where one exists. | The owner finds 50 − 5 easier than halving 90, and many learners will too. |
| 27 Nov 2026 | Division added as version 2, with its own grid and levels. | Knowing 7 × 8 does not always mean knowing 56 ÷ 7. |
| 27 Nov 2026 | Dividing by 12: guess from the tens digit, check with "10 lots plus 2 lots", go down if too much. | The owner's suggestion. "Ask: 12 times what makes 84?" only helps someone who already knows the 12s. |
| 27 Nov 2026 | Build on eStage, in a new project of its own (80750), not inside the eBiz123s hub. | The audience is parents, not hub members. A separate project keeps sign-ups, payments and children's data apart. |
| 27 Nov 2026 | Working name: The Gold Grid. | Named after what children are chasing, and not tied to times tables. |
| 27 Nov 2026 | Treat this as a project, not a "to consider" idea. | The owner's call, before any more building. |
